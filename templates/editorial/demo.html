Ink Is a Technology

Five centuries before the interface, the printed page solved hierarchy, rhythm and restraint. The screens that replaced it are still catching up.

By Ada Vane · Plates by R. Okafor · 18 min read

A page of body text is a machine for reading. The printer knew this before the designer did: measure the line to sixty-some characters, set the leading a little loose, and let the columns do the talking. Everything on this page — the drop cap, the rule between columns, the pulled quote — is that machine, uncovered.

The grid is not a cage; it is the score the page plays. Twelve columns on the features, two on the essays, one on the editorial — and a hairline wherever two things must not be mistaken for each other.

White space is not empty. It is the paper, holding its ground against the ink.

Red appears exactly where the reader’s eye needs a handle: the issue stamp, the section kicker, the folio number, the byline rule. Four appearances, one meaning — start here.

And the shadows? There are none. Print has no light source, so the page earns its depth from alignment alone. If an element needs a shadow to be found, it has already failed the composition.

01 — Controls

Ink rectangles, red asks

Buttons are square-cornered ink rectangles with caption-sans labels; the seal-red one is the page’s only real ask. Inputs are a single underline on paper, never a box, and focus turns the rule red.

The accent token is ink black by design — in a print system the accent is the ink itself, and keeping it black leaves red as the only color the eye can be steered by.

  • Columns fold to oneThe two-column body with its column rule becomes a single 68-character column. The hairline survives as the rule between stacked sections — it is the last piece of the grid standing.
  • Headlines step, never swapDisplay headlines go 84px → 30–34px and stay in the display serif at tight leading. Switching to sans "for small screens" ends the identity right there.
  • The masthead compressesThe wordmark scales down and keeps the 3px ink rule beneath it; the red stamp shrinks into a rotated chip at the right of the bar so the issue is still stamped on every scroll top.
  • Drop cap trimmed, keptThe display drop cap goes from 74px to ~46px. It costs nothing on touch and is the strongest "you are reading a magazine" signal the page has.
  • Captions under plates, alwaysPlates keep their white sheet, square corners and "Plate n." caption below; on a narrow column the caption wraps to two lines rather than truncating.
  • Targets grow, rules stay 1pxContents rows become ≥52px tall and the continue button rises to 34px. Hairlines stay exactly 1px and shadows stay exactly none.
  • Hover becomes tapNav highlights and quiet underlined links are pointer affordances; on touch, the red kicker and the stamped bar carry the state. Nothing on the page depends on hover to be operable.
