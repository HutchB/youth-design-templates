01 — Color roles

A gray canvas, white cards, one tint

The page is grouped background gray and the cards are white; everything else is ink or secondary ink. Blue marks interaction — links, selected controls, the single filled action. Indigo exists for a genuinely second purpose and appears as rarely as blue.

02 — Shape & depth

Depth is luminance, plus blur for the bars

Cards float because the canvas around them is gray — not because a shadow lifts them. In dark mode the same idea becomes a brightness ramp: the window darkens, layers step up. Shadows are kept for things that genuinely float, like sheets and popovers.

Window base#000000
Grouped card#1C1C1E
Elevated / input#2C2C2E
Navbar material — rgba(249,249,249,0.75) + blur(20px) saturate(180%)
Dark material — rgba(30,30,30,0.75), same blur recipe

Bars blur whatever scrolls beneath them; content never stops abruptly at a bar edge. Radii: 8 for small controls, 12–16 for cards and menus, 20–28 for sheets.

03 — Controls

Platform shapes, never restyled

Switches are green when on with a white thumb; segmented controls raise a white segment from a gray track; rows keep a 44pt minimum with a trailing value and chevron. Buttons come in filled, tinted and plain — one filled button per view.

05 — Mobile

This surface is already mobile — so scale the type, not the rules

The group list survives every size change intact: rows grow with Dynamic Type, the large title collapses into a blurred bar, and the gray canvas keeps breathing through the group gaps. Nothing gets smaller to fit; nothing gets rounder or louder.

  • Touch targetsRows are 44pt at the default size and only grow from there. Icons and chevrons sit inside the row's target — no separate smaller hit areas.
  • Dynamic TypeWhen the user's text size grows, rows stretch and sub-lines wrap; the list re-flows in a single column instead of shrinking type below Caption2.
  • Collapsed barOn scroll the large title gives way to the blurred material bar with a centered 17px title — content keeps sliding under the translucent layer.
  • No hoverHover highlights do not exist on touch; the pressed state is a ~10% foreground fill on the row for as long as the finger stays down.
  • Regular widthOn iPad the same interface becomes a split view: the list docks left at ~320pt and detail slides in at the right. The rules do not change with it.
  • Safe areasStatus and home-indicator zones reserve their insets at every size; the blurred bars let content pass beneath rather than stopping at the edge.

06 — Off-style, for contrast

What breaks the platform feel

Shadow as hierarchy 24px radius, a heavy soft glow, blue title on a white card floating over white. Shadowed cards over a same-color page erase the grouped canvas — the system's whole depth cue is background contrast, not elevation.
Background as hierarchy 10px radius, no shadow, hairline between rows. The card reads as raised because the gray canvas shows around it; in dark mode the same job is done by the #1C1C1E step.
Blue everywhereBlue titles, blue labels, blue dividers…
Blue values, blue icons, blue buttons in pairs. When every pixel is the tint, nothing reads as tappable and nothing reads as text.
Blue means interactive
Labels are ink, values are secondary ink — only Sign Out and the switch are tinted. One tint per role: blue marks the things a finger can act on, gray carries everything the eye reads.

Also out: downloaded fonts, gradients on controls, rows under 44pt, and decorative color that is neither ink nor a platform tint.
