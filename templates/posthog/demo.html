01 — Color

Cream paper, one yellow action

The page is never white — the cream canvas runs behind everything and white cards float on it. Yellow-orange is spent on exactly one action per view; every selected or active state falls back to ink, and blue handles links and informational marks.

02 — Reading data

Charts behave like the rest of the page

Bars are flat color on hairline grids — no gradients, no glow, no 3D. Series draw from ink and blue; every number is tabular so columns don't jitter as values tick. Drop-off between funnel steps is the only place red appears.

Event names, user ids and timestamps are always monospace; percentages always carry a sign. If a chart needs a third series, it takes a tint of an existing color — not a new hue.

03 — Mobile

The dashboard folds, the paper stays

On phones the KPI row becomes a two-up grid, the chart keeps its ink-and-blue bars at half height, and the funnel collapses to the two steps that matter. Cream canvas and hairline cards survive every breakpoint untouched.

04 — Off-style, for contrast

What breaks the sketchbook

Floating gradient card White-to-yellow gradient fill · heavy drop shadow · oversized radius. Shadows and washes make the page feel like a template, not a sketchbook, and a second yellow surface competes with the CTA.
Same content, on-style Flat white card, 1px olive hairline, 8px radius, cream visible around it. Structure comes from the border; the only loud thing on screen is still the button.

Also out: pure-white page backgrounds, dark hero bands, glass effects, and pastel callout tints used as product-card fills — those belong to long-form docs only.
